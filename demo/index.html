What is a factorial cumulant?

The rth factorial cumulant is r! times the coefficient of tr in log E[(1+t)X]. The first is the mean; the second is variance minus mean. Higher orders describe further features of the count law. They are different from ordinary cumulants.

This pair demonstrates that order D + 1 can be necessary. The universal theorem proves that orders 2, …, D + 1 suffice for every admissible law on {0, …, D}. Comparing this one pair is an illustration of sharpness, not a proof of the universal theorem.

03 / Recovery

Work backwards.
Recover a simple source.

For a Bernoulli source, a single higher factorial cumulant determines the probability of a source count. The mean then reveals the background rate.

Beyond the demonstration

An exact result.
A precise boundary.

The theorem concerns exact population cumulants. Finite samples introduce uncertainty; detector response, random background rates and dependence require separate analysis. No physical system is validated here.

What about sample size and uncertainty?

The repository includes a conditional Bernoulli confidence calculation. Its conservative intervals can be wide even at large sample sizes. Exact identification does not promise an efficient estimator or practical measurement protocol.
